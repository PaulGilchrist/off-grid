Beginner's Guide to Making the Cables

You've never fabricated cables before — this walks through every part, tool, and joint. Practice on 6-inch scrap offcuts of each size before touching the real runs (buy 2–3 ft extra per size for practice and mistakes).

1. Vocabulary

3. Anatomy of a proper crimp — the 6 rules

  1. Right size, matched set. Lug marked "1/0" gets 1/0 cable and the die sized for that barrel. No improvising.
  2. Strip the right length. Most lugs have an insulation stop inside the barrel: insulation flush at the entrance, copper filling the rest. Too long = exposed copper; too short = insulation inside the crimp (bad connection).
  3. Fully insert, no frays. Every strand goes in the barrel. If they splay, re-strip — never cut strands to make it "fit."
  4. Hex crimp, two dents, rotated. One hex squeeze near the conductor side, rotate 180°, squeeze again near the stud side. The tool must bottom out — if it closes easily, the die or lug size is wrong.
  5. Adhesive-lined shrink over every crimp. Slide the tubing on before crimping (the classic rookie mistake), covering barrel + ¾" of intact insulation each side; heat middle-outward until glue beads at both ends.
  6. Test, then torque. Multimeter continuity (beep, near 0 Ω), tug test (20+ lb), bolt with star washer, torque to spec, then a paint-pen mark across lug-to-busbar so you can spot rotation later.

5. Cable recipes — make these one at a time

Recipe A — Battery links & main leads (W1/W2/W3/W13): 2/0 cable, two-hole lugs

  1. Route string, add 10%, mark, cut with the cable cutter.
  2. Slide adhesive-lined shrink (big enough to swallow the lug barrel) onto the cable. First!
  3. Strip ~1" with a knife: shallow spiral score, snap, pull. All strands full-length and clean.
  4. Insert copper fully into the two-hole lug until insulation hits the barrel stop.
  5. Hydraulic tool, correct die: hex squeeze near barrel mouth, rotate 180°, squeeze near the holes. Tool must close completely.
  6. Shrink over the whole barrel until tight, glue beading at both ends.
  7. Continuity probe lug-hole to far end (beep, near zero). Tug test. Label the cable's route (e.g., "BAT1− → BAT2+ SERIES").
  8. Make the links: 2 × 20" series (BAT1−→BAT2+, BAT3−→BAT4+), 1 × 24" + tie (BAT1+→BAT3+), 1 × 24" − tie (BAT2−→BAT4−), plus mains W1/W2/W13.

Recipe B — Branch DC cables (W4/W5/W7/W8/W9/W22/W23): 4–8 AWG, ring terminals

  1. Measure, cut, slide on ¾–1" adhesive-lined shrink.
  2. Strip ⅜" with strippers; twist strands clockwise so they stay tight.
  3. Thread the ring terminal's insulation funnel over the jacket; copper into the barrel.
  4. Ratchet crimper: one firm squeeze until the ratchet releases (matching die for bare barrels).
  5. Shrink, continuity, tug test. ANL fuses clamp the ring with their own studs + star washers.

Recipe C — Portable solar run (W6): 10 AWG PV wire, MC4 ends

💡
Your panels already have MC4 pigtails — you're only making the extension from the RV inlet to MPPT #2. Use UV-rated double-insulated PV wire, not auto wire.
  1. Easiest: buy a pre-made 10 AWG MC4 extension (20 ft, male+female). Or make it: strip 7 mm, insert into each MC4 contact (copper visible in the contact window), crimp with the MC4 tool, push the crimped contact into the housing until it clicks and the O-ring seats.
  2. Tug each wire 15 lb — it must not pull from the housing.
  3. At MPPT #2, the bare tinned stranded ends clamp directly into PV+ / PV− (accepts up to 4 AWG stranded); bootlace ferrules + shrink if you want it tidy; torque ≈ 1.5 N·m (13 in-lb).
  4. Mount the inlet so mated connectors hang down with a drip loop — never let them sit in puddles.

Recipe D — AC pigtails (W11/W12): 6 AWG THHN in the Power Center

  1. Shore unplugged, generator off. Photograph the Power Center before touching anything.
  2. Cut three 5-ft lengths: black (L1), red (L2), white (N) — keep house color code.
  3. Strip ½"; twist clockwise; ferrule or tin optional under screw clamps.
  4. Land on MultiPlus AC-IN 1 (L, L, N) and AC-OUT (L, L, N) per the unit diagram; torque to markings.
  5. Route through existing knockouts with grommets; clamp against chafe.
  6. Continuity map before power: beep out L1/L2/N panel-lug → MultiPlus to prove no crossed pairs; confirm open (OL) from any hot to chassis.

Recipe E — Engine-sense (W10) and small wire

  1. 14 AWG with an insulated ring or Scotchlok T-tap at an ignition-switched truck fuse (test first: 12 V only with key ON).
  2. Inline ATO 5 A holder within 6" of the truck side; route with hitch articulation slack, unpluggable at the RV.

6. Assembly order & polarity discipline

  • Colors: red = DC+, black = DC−, white = AC neutral, black/red THHN = AC hots. Label both ends of every cable with its ID (W1…W19) before routing.
  • One cable at a time. Never leave a live battery terminal loose — a dropped 2/0 on the chassis throws a welding-arc flash. Battery negative disconnects first, reconnects last with the disconnect switch OFF.
  • First-connection ritual: negatives → positive main through the (OFF) disconnect → switch ON → SmartShunt shows voltage → enable MultiPlus, MPPTs, Orion one at a time, watching the shunt.

Battery Tray — Sized for the Vatrers

Battery: 15.12"D × 7.64"W × 9.76"H, M8 top terminals. Factory GC27 slots (~13.5" × 6.7" × 9.4") cannot take them — build a flat tray.

LayoutTray footprint (batteries + ¾" gaps + 1" lug clearance)Best for
Single row ×3≈ 26" W × 17" D × 11" HBasement bay between frames, low & centered
Single row ×4≈ 34" W × 17" D × 11" HPass-through storage (check door clearance)
2×2 block ×4≈ 16.5" W × 32" D × 11" HIf width is tight but depth is free
  • 2S2P layout: keep each series pair (BAT1+BAT2, BAT3+BAT4) adjacent so the 2/0 series links stay short; equalize both pairs to 100% before paralleling.
  • Platform: ¾" plywood or HDPE, sealed/painted; two stainless hold-downs per battery across the top (not just front).
  • Keep terminals reachable from front/top — you'll torque-check after 500 miles.
  • Weight: confirm per-battery on the Vatrer sheet (typ. ~46–60 lb class); 4 × 60 lb ≈ 240 lb replacing ~120 lb of AGMs — re-weigh at a scale after the swap.
  • Leave vent clearance above terminals — LFP vents gas under fault; don't seal into an airtight box.

Install Instructions

⚠️
Safety first: shore unplugged, generator off, battery disconnect OFF, WFCO AC breaker OPEN before any AC work. No rings or watches. DC side: negative off first, on last (with disconnect switch OFF). All DC fuses within 18" of battery positive. A 24 V bank can arc hard — treat every 2/0 as live until proven dead with a multimeter. If the 50 A panel splice (Phase 3, step 3) feels intimidating, any RV electrician will do that half-hour of work — it protects you and the Brinkley warranty.

Phase 0 — Verify (½ day, before buying cable)

  1. Confirm gauge/termination points of the factory inverter-prep cables (need ≥ 2/0 to reuse W1; if lighter, run your own). Locate the generator transfer relay and inverter-prep splice (①). Photograph everything.
  2. Locate the WFCO's AC feed breaker in the Power Center — it will be taped OFF and its DC output cut + capped (⑧). The MultiPlus-II takes over charging; the Orion IP67 takes over the 12 V loads.
  3. Plan the truck run: route 1 AWG from the truck battery along the frame to the hitch, mount the MRBF 125 A within 18" of the positive post, and pick a weather-protected spot for the Anderson SB175 inlet on the RV front wall.
  4. Pull string through the roof→basement conduit; pick the MC4 inlet location in pass-through storage; plan to seal the penetration.
  5. Measure the chosen tray spot against the footprint table; email Brinkley service for warranty position; keep all factory parts boxed.

Phase 1 — Practice weekend (1 evening)

  1. Buy 3 ft each of 2/0, 4 AWG, and 10 AWG PV offcuts + spare lugs/rings/MC4s.
  2. Run Recipes A, B, C on scrap. Saw practice crimps open: copper should look cold-forged into the barrel — no voids, no cut strands.
  3. Only after 3–4 clean practice crimps per size, build the real cables.

Phase 2 — Battery bank + tray (1 day)

  1. Build/mount the tray (footprint table), strap batteries in two pairs (BAT1+BAT2 = pair A, BAT3+BAT4 = pair B), keep terminal access.
  2. Install 400 A disconnect + Class T 300 A on pair-A + (BAT1+) within 18".
  3. Series-pair first: BAT1−→BAT2+ and BAT3−→BAT4+ (2/0 series links). Each pair reads ≈ 25.6 V. Charge both pairs to 100% and confirm within 0.1 V of each other before paralleling.
  4. Parallel the pairs: + tie BAT1+→BAT3+ (→ + busbar), − tie BAT2−→BAT4−. Torque M8 ≈ 70 in-lb (verify Vatrer label).
  5. W13: BAT4− → SmartShunt → − busbar. All returns — existing 12 V loads (W14) and the Orion IP67 − common — land on the − busbar so the shunt meters everything.
  6. Set Vatrer BMS series mode per manual (2S per pair, pairs in parallel), self-heat on; pair the BT apps.

Phase 3 — MultiPlus-II (1–2 days, core job)

  1. Mount upright, ≥ 4" air clearance, fan/grille if enclosed.
  2. DC: W1/W2 lugs to MultiPlus terminals, torque ≈ 12 N·m / 105 in-lb (verify label); insulated box spanner.
  3. AC (①): move shore/gen feed from panel main lugs → AC-IN 1 (W11); AC-OUT → panel main lugs (W12). Factory bonding stays; never create a second neutral-ground bond.
  4. WFCO (⑧): open + tape its AC breaker OFF, cut its DC output, cap both ends, and land the Orion IP67 OUT + (ANL 100 A, W24) on the 12 V fuse panel feed it used to supply. The two 12 V supplies must never touch the same bus.
  5. VictronConnect: LiFePO4 24 V per Vatrer label (absorb 28.8 / float 27.6 / storage 26.4); charger 70 A; shore limit 50 A; generator limit 40 A; low-batt alarm 24.0 V; PowerAssist ON; SmartShunt paired (600 Ah).
  6. Plug the VE.Bus Smart dongle into the MultiPlus COM port (RJ45) — from then on the MultiPlus is visible over Bluetooth in VictronConnect. Form the VE.Smart network (MPPT #1, MPPT #2, Orion XS, SmartShunt, dongle — one creates, the rest join, same password). Keep the MK3-USB in the toolbox for MultiPlus firmware updates.

Phase 4 — MPPT #2 + MC4 inlet (½ day)

  1. Mount MPPT #2 beside #1 (④), ventilated ≥ 10 cm above/below.
  2. Install MC4 bulkhead inlet (⑤), sealed, connectors facing down with a drip loop.
  3. Build/install W6 (Recipe C) through the conduit to MPPT #2 PV terminals.
  4. W4/W5 output → +busbar (ANL 60 A) / −busbar. Configure identical to MPPT #1; pair both in VictronConnect — two harvesters, one view.
  5. Plug in the portable series pair only with panels shaded or the MPPT DC switch off. Verify series Voc < 100 V cold (2× ~41 V ≈ 82 V: OK).

Phase 5 — DC-DC: Orion XS 1400 (truck) + Orion IP67 (12 V loads) (1 day)

  1. Truck side (⑥): MRBF 125 A within 18" of the truck battery +, 1 AWG run along the frame to the hitch, Anderson SB175 half there; engine-sense (W10) to an ignition-switched fuse (test first), ATO 5 A in line.
  2. RV side: mount the Anderson inlet on the front wall (drip loop, connectors facing down). W7: inlet → Orion XS +IN, 4 AWG, ANL 60 A at the inlet. W8: XS output → 24 V + busbar, 4 AWG, ANL 60 A. W9 chassis bond.
  3. Configure the XS in VictronConnect: LiFePO4 24 V (absorb 28.8 / float 27.6), input limit 50 A — that's ≈ 24 A into the bank at 28 V (12 V input can't push the full 50 A out; the input limit caps it). Remote on/off to the engine-sense lead.
  4. Orion IP67 24/12-100: mount near the bank (finned case needs airflow). IN + 4 AWG from the + busbar (ANL 60 A, W22); IN − common 4 AWG to the − busbar on the load side of the shunt (W23); OUT + 2 AWG (ANL 100 A, W24) to the 12 V panel feed. Verify ≈ 12 V out before landing the panel.
  5. Highway test with the clamp meter: expect ~20–24 A into the bank; ~4–5 kWh per 8 h tow day. A top-up, not a fast charger.

Amazon shopping notes

  • Welding cable: Amazon sells Class K welding cable cut-to-length (2/0, 1 AWG, 4/2 AWG) — order the purchase-list lengths plus slack; check it's flexible stranded, not solid.
  • Victron gear: confirm the seller is an authorized Victron reseller (warranty); the VE.Bus Smart dongle, MK3-USB, Orion XS, and IP67 are all on Amazon.
  • Vatrer: confirm the listing is the self-heating version with BT and M8 studs — the non-heated variant can't be series-mixed with heated units.
  • Fuses/lugs: Class T, MRBF, ANL, Anderson SB175, and 2/0 two-hole lugs are all Amazon staples — buy one spare of each fuse value.
